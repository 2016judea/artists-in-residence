# Lewis Hine — the measured visual language

Measured 2026-11-20 off Credo No. 2's eleven photographs (Empire State Building 1930–31, work portraits 1920–37).
The Library of Congress catalog adds his National Child Labor Committee photographs (1908–24).

- **Large-format negative, silver gelatin print.** Full tonal range, blacks deep but not crushed, fine grain,
  slightly soft corners. Skies often nearly blank; the city below goes grey in haze.
- **On the steel with the men.** One to three ironworkers in caps, overalls and gloves (no hard hats, no
  harnesses), at ease at a dizzying height; tilted horizons; bodies and girders as dark shapes against pale sky.
- **Work portraits share the frame between the worker and the machine**: rivets, belts, threads, housings seen as
  carefully as forearms and faces. Window or single-lamp light, raking; eyes on the work.
- **Dignity, competence, concentration.** Never pity, never a pose for the camera (the young structural workers
  smiling are the exception that proves it).
- **Rule for anything generated in his manner:** no caption, place, date or person name, so nothing made here can
  pass for a historical record. His real captions belong only to his real photographs.
