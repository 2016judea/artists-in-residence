# Santiago Ramón y Cajal — the measured visual language

Measured 2026-11-20 off eleven figures from *Histologie du système nerveux de l'homme & des vertébrés* (1909),
Aetas Nova No. 3's art. Provenance: `featured.json` (archive.org pages). The `-st` stencils in the issue are not used.

- **Pen and India ink, reproduced as a line block on cream book paper.** Black or nothing: no wash, no hatching
  (one figure, 116, adds a second printing in red; it is the exception).
- **Golgi cells are solid black silhouettes.** An irregular body (pyramidal, star, pear) throws dendrites that taper
  and fork again and again, the finest twigs beaded and bristling with spines. The axon is one fine line of constant
  width that wanders far, giving off side branches.
- **Line weight runs from heavy bodies to hairlines** inside one cell.
- **Low-power sections map a whole region at once**: fibre bundles as dense fields of fine parallel lines sweeping in
  curved streams, cell groups as stippled ovals and dots, the tissue's own cut edge as the figure's outline.
- **The field is cropped like a window**: cells run off the edge, stray fibres cross bare paper.
- **Labels are single printed letters** (A–L, a–e) beside the part, some with a thin straight leader.
- **Never:** colour, arrows (his circuit diagrams have them; these figures don't), a microscope's round vignette,
  scale bars, captions, organelle diagrams.
