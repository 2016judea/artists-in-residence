Use the attached figures strictly as the style and material anchor. Reproduce their exact rendering language: a figure from an early-twentieth-century histology atlas of the nervous system, a low-power drawing of a whole region of a stained section (a nucleus, a ganglion, a horn of the spinal cord, a layer of cortex) in pen and India ink, reproduced as a line block on cream book paper. Fibre bundles are dense fields of fine parallel lines that sweep, fan and cross in curved streams; groups of cell bodies are clusters of small stippled ovals and dark dots; the matrix between them is fine stipple and short wavy strokes; here and there a few larger cells are drawn whole in solid black with their branching dendrites. The section has its own outline, a curved cut edge of tissue or a straight edge where the drawing simply stops, and sits on bare paper. Contrast comes from the density of marks, never from wash. Exact, patient and observed: a whole region mapped at once.
